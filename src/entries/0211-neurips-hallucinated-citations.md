**Summary:** GPTZero scanned 4,841 of the 5,290 accepted papers and said each flagged citation was checked by a person. According to Fortune, some blended pieces of real papers into believable-sounding titles and author lists, while others were made up entirely, with nonexistent authors and fabricated titles. That's about 1% of papers, and TechCrunch noted a bad citation doesn't necessarily mean the research itself is invalid. Still, it was an awkward look for the field building the tools.
